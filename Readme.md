# Evaluating Traffic Law Enforcement Patterns, Driver Demographics and Citations





## Objective



The goal of this project is to analyze traffic stop logs to evaluate law enforcement patterns, driver demographics, search rates and citation outcomes. It aims to provide data driven insights into enforcement distribution, operational focus areas and search effectiveness





### Business Problem



Public safety agencies, government agencies and analysts need a clear, data-driven insights into traffic enforcement operations to ensure accountability, resource efficiency and equitable enforcement.





#### Key Goals:



* Identifying peak enforcement windows to better align officer deployments with higher violation timeframe.
* Measure the proportion of traffic searches that result in citations, arrests or contraband seizures versus unproductive stops.
* Quantifying demographic distribution across traffic violations, search rates and stop outcomes to promote transparency  and fair police practices.





#### Key areas of Analysis:



* Police behaviour analysis
* Demographics categorization
* Disparity among searches and arrests
* Categorized stops analysis





#### Tools used



* Microsoft Excel - Data exploration
* Jupyter Notebook - Data cleaning, Missing values handling and creating Calculated fields.
* Microsoft SQL Server – Data extraction and analysis
* Microsoft PowerPoint – Business presentation and reporting





#### Key Insights \& Recommendations



* Out of the 91,260 total traffic stops, officers only conducted 3,187 searches. Roughly 3.5% searches. Therefore even though stop volume is high, the search is relatively selective. Out of those searches about 1/4 come out to be drugs related.



* It is observed that the age group 21-40 represents the vast majority of stops while drivers below age 20 and above age 60 are very less. Even though the majority of stops were of white people, the arrest rate of black people was more than any other ethnic group, followed by Hispanic people. This shows that the police is biased towards the minority groups and arrest them more than others.



* About 60% of all traffic stops stem from over speeding violations followed by Moving violations and equipment violations. From these stops, the majority of speeding violations end up in citations whereas moving violations and equipment violations end up in higher secondary warnings or searches relative to their total volume



* The hourly trends show distinct spikes in search frequency and drug related stops during late evening and early morning hours (between 22:00 and 03:00). While daytime stop volume is high due to high commuter density, nighttime stops carry a significant higher probability of search executions.



##### 

##### Recommendations



* Establish stricter protocols or superiors approval requirements for discretionary searches during routine speeding or equipment violation stops. It will minimize low-yield searches and maintain high search hit rates on severe offenses.
* Ensure equitable enforcement standards across driver groups. This will help departments to defend patrol practices with objective, transparent data.
* Late night searches should be increased for severe offenses and benchmark standards and targets should be established.
